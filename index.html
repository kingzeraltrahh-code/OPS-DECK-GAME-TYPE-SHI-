OPS DECK — How To Play

GM runs the world. Players describe actions. Avrae rolls every die. OPS DECK tracks the result.

1. The Core Loop

  1. GM describes the situation.
  2. Player says what they want to do.
  3. If failure is uncertain and matters, GM calls for a roll.
  4. Player rolls in Discord with Avrae.
  5. Compare the result to the DC.
  6. GM applies the consequence and updates the dashboard.

If something is obvious, do not roll. If the rules do not cover something, the GM makes a reasonable ruling.

2. Checks

Roll: 1d20 + the relevant modifier.

  • DC 5 — Very Easy
  • DC 10 — Easy
  • DC 12 — Moderate
  • DC 15 — Hard
  • DC 18 — Very Hard
  • DC 20 — Extreme

Meet or beat the DC = success. Below the DC = failure.

Natural 20: critical success. Natural 1: critical failure/complication. A critical failure should create a problem, not automatically end the game.

3. Characters

Characters track HP, AC, Attack Bonus, STR, DEX, INT, WIS, skills, abilities, equipment and class.

  • STR: force, melee, lifting, breaking.
  • DEX: stealth, movement, dodging, ranged attacks.
  • INT: hacking, investigation, technology, puzzles.
  • WIS: perception, medicine, survival, danger.

Normal trained skill bonus: +2. Highly trained: +3. GM decides the appropriate skill.

4. Advantage / Disadvantage

Advantage = roll 2d20 and keep the higher. Disadvantage = roll 2d20 and keep the lower.

/r 2d20kh1+MOD   /r 2d20kl1+MOD

5. Combat

Combat begins when the situation needs turn order. Roll initiative in Avrae:

/r 1d20+DEX

Highest initiative goes first. On a turn, a character may move, attack, use an ability, use an item, or help.

Attack: /r 1d20+AttackBonus. If the result meets or beats the enemy AC, it hits. Then roll damage in Avrae.

  • Knife — 1d4
  • Pistol — 1d6
  • Rifle — 1d8
  • Shotgun — 1d10
  • Heavy Weapon — 1d12
  • Improvised Weapon — 1d4

Combat is optional. Players can hide, escape, negotiate, disable enemies, or finish the objective and leave.

6. HP & Down

At 0 HP, a character is DOWN and normally cannot act until helped. A teammate can attempt DC 10 Medicine; on success, the Down character returns to 1 HP.

7. Movement & Rooms

OPS DECK uses room-to-room movement, not a 5-foot grid. A player moves along a connected map route. Normally, moving to an adjacent room is the movement for that turn.

The GM may allow extra movement when the scene is safe, or require a check when movement is risky.

8. Security / Scenario Meter

In Heist, the meter is Security. It is a live 0–10 dashboard meter. The GM can use the −1 / +1 / 0 / MAX controls to change it. Raise it for failed stealth, alarms, camera detection, escaped guards and excessive noise; lower it when players disable cameras, bypass security, deal with guards quietly, or complete special objectives.

  • 0–2 — Calm
  • 3–4 — Suspicious
  • 5–6 — Alert
  • 7–8 — Lockdown
  • 9 — Critical
  • 10 — Final Lockdown

Final Lockdown is a consequence state, not an automatic "you lose" button. The GM decides the final complication, escape pressure, reinforcements or objective change.

9. Other Modes

Haunted House: the meter is Dread. It rises from failed supernatural checks, cursed objects, major supernatural events and separation.

World Collapse: the meter is Stability. It tracks how close the situation is to collapse as supplies, survival, disasters, enemies and objectives affect the situation.

10. Items & Inventory

  • Medkit: restore 1d6 HP using Avrae.
  • Master Key: automatically opens one compatible lock.
  • EMP Grenade: disables electronic devices in the affected area.
  • Security Badge: allows access to restricted areas.

Reasonably small items can normally be carried. No complicated weight calculation is required; GM decides when something is excessive.

11. Objectives

Missions can have a Main Objective, Optional Objectives, and a Hidden Objective. Completing the objective does not necessarily end the mission; players may still need to escape or survive.

12. Secrets

Secret rooms, hidden objectives, hidden events and GM-only information are not shown to players until discovered. The GM decides what clue or action reveals them.

13. Failure

Failure should usually change the situation instead of stopping the story. Examples: an alarm activates, a guard notices the player, a door locks, an enemy appears, time runs out, or the scenario meter increases.

14. Avrae Is The Dice Engine

OPS DECK never decides a random player outcome. Use Avrae for:

  • Checks: /r 1d20+MOD
  • Attack: /r 1d20+ATTACK
  • Damage: /r 1d8+MOD
  • Initiative: /r 1d20+DEX
  • Advantage: /r 2d20kh1+MOD
  • Disadvantage: /r 2d20kl1+MOD

OPS DECK = map, mission, characters, enemies, items, meters and tracking. Avrae = dice. GM = final ruling.

15. Quick Start

  1. Choose Heist, Haunted House, or World Collapse.
  2. Choose difficulty.
  3. Create characters.
  4. Generate the mission.
  5. GM describes the opening scene.
  6. Players choose actions.
  7. Roll only when necessary, using Avrae.
  8. Track HP, enemies, loot, movement and the scenario meter.
  9. Complete the objective.
  10. Escape or survive.
